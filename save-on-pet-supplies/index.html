Food and treats

  • Compare the cost per day, not the bag price.
  • Use repeat-delivery discounts for food you buy regularly, but check you can pause or cancel easily.
  • Weigh portions. Overfeeding wastes money and can lead to weight problems and vet bills later.
  • Treats add up quickly: use part of the daily food allowance as training rewards.

Vet care

  • Prevention is cheaper than cure: vaccinations, parasite control and dental care help avoid bigger bills.
  • Vet health plans spread routine costs monthly and often include discounts on other treatment.
  • Ask for an estimate before non-urgent treatment, and ask about options.
  • Charity vets such as PDSA and Blue Cross (UK) and low-cost clinics run by shelters (US) help eligible owners on low incomes.

Supplies and accessories

  • Buy adult-size crates with dividers and adjustable harnesses so you don’t have to replace them as a puppy grows.
  • Choose beds with washable, replaceable covers.
  • Seasonal sales, such as Black Friday, are a good time for bigger items like beds, crates and cat trees.
  • Pre-loved kit (crates, carriers, hutches) can be good value. Clean and disinfect it thoroughly.

Services

  • Learn to brush, clip nails and clean teeth at home to stretch the time between grooming appointments.
  • Book kennels and catteries early for school holidays and Christmas, when they fill up.
  • Ask local walkers and sitters about regular-booking rates.

Frequently asked questions

What is a vet health plan?
A monthly payment plan offered by many vet practices that covers routine care such as vaccinations, check-ups and parasite treatments. It isn’t insurance, so it won’t cover accidents or illness.
Are pet food subscriptions cheaper?
Repeat-delivery discounts can make regular buys cheaper, but compare the subscribed price with other retailers and check the cancellation terms.
