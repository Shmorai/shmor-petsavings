Pet food compared: types, labels and the real cost

Food is the biggest regular cost for most pet owners. Here’s how to compare dog and cat food fairly, read the label and find savings without compromising on nutrition.

Types of pet food

TypeGood to know
Dry (kibble)Usually the lowest cost per day. Keeps well once opened. Easy to measure and use in puzzle feeders.
Wet (cans, pouches, trays)Higher moisture, which can help cats who drink little. Usually costs more per day than dry.
Mixed feedingPart dry, part wet. Reduce each portion so the total matches your pet’s daily needs.
Fresh / cooked subscriptionsDelivered, portioned meals, often frozen or chilled. Convenient but usually the most expensive option.
RawRaw meat diets carry a risk of bacteria such as Salmonella for pets and people. Talk to your vet first and follow strict hygiene.

“Complete” vs “complementary”: read the label first

The most important words on the pack tell you whether the food can be fed on its own:

  • US: look for an AAFCO nutritional adequacy statement saying the food is “complete and balanced” for your pet’s life stage (growth, adult maintenance or all life stages).
  • UK and EU: a “complete” food provides everything your pet needs on its own. A “complementary” food (many treats, toppers and some wet foods) must be fed alongside a complete food.

Our guide to reading pet food labels explains ingredient lists, naming rules and feeding guides in more detail.

Feed for life stage and size

  • Puppies and kittens need food formulated for growth. Large-breed puppies often need a large-breed puppy food to support steady growth.
  • Adults need an adult maintenance food matched to their activity level.
  • Seniors may benefit from different calorie levels or textures. Ask your vet if your pet has health conditions.
  • Special diets (kidney, urinary, weight loss, allergy) are often prescription or veterinary diets. Don’t switch without your vet’s advice.

How to work out the real cost per day

  1. Find the daily feeding amount for your pet’s weight on the pack (in grams or cups).
  2. Divide the pack size by the daily amount to get the number of days it lasts.
  3. Divide the price by the number of days. That’s your cost per day.

A cheaper bag isn’t always cheaper to feed. Energy-dense foods often have smaller daily portions, so compare cost per day, not price per kilo or pound.

Ways to pay less for pet food

  • Subscribe and save: many online pet stores discount repeat deliveries. Check you can change or pause the order easily.
  • Buy bigger bags if your pet will finish it before the best-before date, and store it in a sealed container.
  • Loyalty schemes at pet stores and supermarkets can add up for regular buys.
  • Avoid overfeeding: weigh portions. It saves money and helps keep your pet at a healthy weight.
  • Change food gradually over about a week when switching to a better-value brand, to avoid upset stomachs.

Frequently asked questions

Is grain-free dog food better?
Not automatically. Most dogs digest grains well, and grain-free diets aren’t necessary unless your vet advises it. In the US, the FDA has investigated a possible link between some grain-free diets and a heart condition (DCM) in dogs, so talk to your vet before choosing one.
Can cats eat dog food?
Not as their main diet. Cats have different nutritional needs, including taurine, so they need a complete cat food.
How do I switch my pet’s food?
Mix a little of the new food into the old, increasing the amount gradually over about 7 days. Speak to your vet if your pet has a sensitive stomach or a medical diet.
