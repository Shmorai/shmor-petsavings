Dog walkers and pet sitters: how to choose safely

You’re handing over your keys and your pet, so a walker or sitter needs to be reliable as well as kind. Here’s what to check.

Photo: Andriyko Podilnyk on Unsplash

Types of service

  • Solo or group dog walks
  • Drop-in visits for cats and small pets: feeding, litter and company
  • House sitting: the sitter stays in your home with your pets
  • Home boarding: your dog stays in the sitter’s home
  • Dog day care: at a centre or a carer’s home

What to check

  • Insurance covering public liability and care, custody and control of your pet, and key cover if they hold your keys.
  • Background checks: a DBS check in the UK, or a background check in the US.
  • Licences: in England, home boarders and dog day care need a council licence. Dog walkers don’t, but some councils limit how many dogs one person can walk in public spaces.
  • Pet first aid training and an emergency plan, including your vet’s details.
  • Professional membership: in the US, groups such as NAPPS and Pet Sitters International offer membership and training.

Questions to ask at a meet-and-greet

  1. How many dogs do you walk at once, and do you walk them on or off lead?
  2. How do you transport dogs, and are they secured in the vehicle?
  3. Will the same person visit each time?
  4. How do you send updates, and what happens if you’re ill?
  5. Can I see your insurance certificate and any licence?

Last updated: 13 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

Do dog walkers need a licence?
Not usually in the UK, but some councils set limits on the number of dogs per walker in public spaces. Home boarding and dog day care need a licence in England.
What insurance should a pet sitter have?
Look for public liability insurance plus care, custody and control cover, which protects pets in their care. Key cover is useful if they hold your keys.
