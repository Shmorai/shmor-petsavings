Dog and cat insurance

Pet insurance compared: what the cover types mean

Pet insurance helps with unexpected vet bills, but policies differ far more than the monthly price suggests. Here’s how to compare cover for dogs and cats.

Types of pet insurance (UK and Europe)

TypeHow it works
LifetimeA vet fee limit each year that refreshes when you renew, so ongoing conditions can stay covered as long as you keep the policy.
Maximum benefitA set amount per condition. Once it’s used up, that condition is no longer covered.
Time-limitedCovers each condition for a set period, often 12 months, then it’s excluded.
Accident-onlyCovers injuries from accidents but not illness. Usually the cheapest option.

How US pet insurance works

Most US plans are accident and illness or accident-only. You usually pay the vet, then claim back. You’ll choose:

  • Reimbursement rate: the share of the eligible bill the plan pays back.
  • Deductible: what you pay before the plan pays. Check whether it’s annual or per condition.
  • Annual limit: the most the plan pays each year (some are unlimited).
  • Wellness add-ons: optional extras for routine care such as vaccines. They aren’t the same as insurance, so check they’re worth the cost.

Pet insurance is regulated by each state. Many states have adopted the NAIC Pet Insurance Model Act, which standardises key definitions and disclosures.

What to check before you buy

  • Vet fee limit: is it per year, per condition or for life?
  • Excess and co-payment: many UK policies add a percentage co-payment once a pet reaches a certain age.
  • Pre-existing conditions: usually excluded. Some insurers may cover a condition after a symptom-free period.
  • Waiting periods: illness cover often starts a few days or weeks after the policy begins.
  • Exclusions: dental, behavioural treatment, hereditary conditions and routine care vary a lot between policies.
  • Age limits: some insurers won’t start a new policy for older pets.
  • Renewal price: premiums usually rise as pets age, so check the price each year.

Ways to keep the cost down

  • Insure while your pet is young and healthy, so fewer conditions are excluded.
  • Choose a higher excess or deductible if you could comfortably afford it.
  • Ask about multi-pet discounts.
  • Compare at renewal, but remember that switching may mean existing conditions aren’t covered by the new insurer.
  • Keep up with vaccinations and parasite control: some policies won’t pay for preventable illness.

If something goes wrong

Complain to the insurer first. In the UK, if you’re not happy with the final response you can take it to the Financial Ombudsman Service for free. In the US, contact your state’s insurance department.

Frequently asked questions

Is pet insurance worth it?
It depends on whether you could pay a large, unexpected vet bill yourself. Insurance spreads the cost, but you’ll still pay the excess and anything above your limits.
Does pet insurance cover pre-existing conditions?
Usually not. Conditions that showed signs before cover started are normally excluded, which is why many owners insure their pet young.
Who regulates pet insurance in the UK?
The Financial Conduct Authority (FCA). Unresolved complaints can go to the Financial Ombudsman Service.
