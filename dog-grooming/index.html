How to choose a dog groomer near you

A good groomer keeps your dog’s coat, skin, nails and ears healthy, and makes the experience calm. Here’s what to look for, whether you choose a salon, a home-based groomer or a mobile van.

  2. What to check

  4. Save money with grooming at home

Salon, home-based or mobile?

  • Salons usually have more equipment and several groomers, but can be busy and noisy.
  • Home-based groomers often work one-to-one, which suits nervous dogs.
  • Mobile groomers come to your door in a fitted van: convenient, and often calmer for anxious or older dogs, but usually priced higher.

What to check

  • Qualifications: in the UK, look for recognised grooming qualifications such as City & Guilds or iPET Network. In the US, look for certification from groups such as the National Dog Groomers Association of America.
  • Insurance: public liability and care, custody and control cover.
  • Drying: ask how dogs are dried. Unattended heated cage dryers can be risky, especially for flat-faced breeds.
  • Handling: will they stop if your dog is stressed? Do they offer short puppy introduction sessions?
  • Vaccinations: reputable groomers usually ask for proof that your dog is up to date.

How often does a dog need grooming?

It depends on the coat. Dogs with continuously growing coats (such as poodles, shih tzus and many doodles) often need a professional groom every 4–8 weeks. Short-coated dogs may only need regular brushing, nail trims and the occasional bath.

Save money with grooming at home

  • Brush between grooms to prevent matting, which can make appointments longer and pricier.
  • Learn to trim nails or ask your groomer for a quick nail-only appointment.
  • Clean teeth with a pet toothpaste: never human toothpaste, which can contain xylitol and is toxic to dogs.

Last updated: 13 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

Do dog groomers need a licence in the UK?
No. Dog grooming isn’t licensed in the UK, which makes checking qualifications, insurance and reviews especially important.
When should a puppy first see a groomer?
Many groomers offer short introduction visits once a puppy has finished its vaccinations. Ask your vet and groomer what they recommend.
