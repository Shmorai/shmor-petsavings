Fireworks and pets: how to keep dogs and cats calm

Bonfire Night, Diwali, New Year’s Eve and the Fourth of July can be frightening for pets. A little preparation makes a big difference.

By the PetSavings editorial team · 13 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Tharun Kumar on Unsplash

On this page

  1. Before fireworks season
  2. On the night
  3. Small pets
  4. When to get help

Before fireworks season

  • Check microchip details are up to date. Frightened pets can bolt. In England, both dogs and cats (over 20 weeks) must be microchipped.
  • Make a safe den: a quiet, covered spot with bedding and familiar toys, in a room away from windows.
  • Talk to your vet early if your pet has been very anxious before. There are treatments and behaviour plans that work best when started in advance.

On the night

  • Walk dogs in daylight, before the fireworks start.
  • Keep cats indoors, with a litter tray, and lock the cat flap.
  • Close curtains and play the TV or music to soften bangs and flashes.
  • Stay calm and let your pet hide if they want to. Don’t force them out.
  • Never take a dog to a fireworks display.

Small pets

Rabbits and guinea pigs living outside can be badly frightened. If you can, bring hutches into a quiet room, shed or garage, or cover part of the hutch with a blanket while leaving ventilation, and add extra bedding to burrow into.

When to get help

Signs of fear include trembling, hiding, panting, pacing, toileting indoors and trying to escape. If your pet is very distressed, or the fear is getting worse each year, speak to your vet. They may refer you to an accredited behaviourist.

Last updated: 13 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

Should I comfort my dog during fireworks?
Yes. Calm comfort doesn’t reinforce fear. Stay relaxed, let your dog come to you and give them a safe place to hide.
Can I give my dog something to calm them?
Only use medication your vet has prescribed for your pet. Never give human medicines, as some are toxic to pets.
