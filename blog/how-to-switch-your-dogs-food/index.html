How to switch your dog's food without upsetting their tummy

Whether you are moving to a different brand, a cheaper bag or a new life-stage recipe, changing your dog's food overnight can cause diarrhoea or put them off eating. UK charities PDSA and Blue Cross both recommend a gradual switch. Here is how it works, and how to stay on budget while you do it.

Why you should change dog food gradually

PDSA, the UK veterinary charity, advises changing food gradually over a couple of weeks to prevent problems. A sudden change can cause tummy upsets or leave a dog refusing to eat. The exception is when your vet tells you to change straight away, for example for a medical diet.

A two-week plan (PDSA)

PDSA's timetable looks like this:

  • Days 1 to 3: give the usual meal, with a small teaspoon of the new food on a separate plate.
  • Days 4 to 10: mix a very small amount of the new food into the old, then slowly increase the new and decrease the old if all goes well.
  • Days 10 to 14: once your dog has eaten the mixed food happily for at least a week, phase out the old food.

A faster one-week plan (Blue Cross)

For a healthy adult dog, Blue Cross suggests a shorter split: about 25% new food on days 1 and 2, 50% on days 3 and 4, 75% on days 5 and 6, and 100% new food from day 7. Puppies, seniors and dogs with sensitive stomachs usually do better with the slower approach, so if in doubt, go slower. Our new puppy checklist covers what to feed and when.

What to do if your dog refuses it or has a loose stool

PDSA says that if a dog will not eat the mix, step back to the last level they accepted and stay there for a few days before trying again. Loose stools can also mean you have moved too fast. If diarrhoea or vomiting is severe, lasts more than a day or two, or comes with tiredness or blood, call your vet, because the change may not be the cause. This is general information, not veterinary advice.

Check the new food, and keep the cost down

Look for a statement that the food is "complete" for your dog's life stage; our label guide explains what the wording means. Because a switch takes one to two weeks, buy a small bag first and keep the old one until you have finished the transition, rather than committing to a large bag you might not use. Frequently asked questions

How long does it take to switch dog food?
PDSA suggests about two weeks, and Blue Cross suggests a quicker seven-day plan for healthy adult dogs. Slower is safer for puppies, seniors and sensitive dogs.
Can I switch my dog's food overnight?
Only if your vet tells you to. Otherwise a sudden change can cause tummy upsets or refusal to eat (PDSA).
What if my dog gets diarrhoea after the change?
Go back to the last mix they handled well and hold it for a few days. If it is severe, lasts more than a day or two or comes with other symptoms, contact your vet.
