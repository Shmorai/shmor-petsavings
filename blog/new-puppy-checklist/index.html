New puppy checklist: what to buy and what to arrange

Bringing a puppy home is exciting and busy. Use this checklist to get the essentials ready, without buying things you don’t need yet.

By the PetSavings editorial team · 13 October 2026 · 1 min read

Photo: Haberdoedas on Unsplash

On this page

  1. Supplies to buy before day one
  2. Admin to arrange
  3. What can wait
  4. The first weeks

Supplies to buy before day one

  • The same puppy food the breeder or rescue was using, to avoid stomach upsets
  • Food and water bowls
  • A collar with an ID tag, plus a lightweight lead
  • A bed with a washable cover, and a crate or pen if you plan to use one
  • Puppy-safe chew toys
  • Poop bags and pet-safe cleaning spray for accidents
  • A soft brush and puppy shampoo
  • A secure way to travel in the car

Admin to arrange

  • Register with a vet and book a health check in the first week or so.
  • Vaccinations: ask your vet about the first course and when your puppy can safely go out.
  • Microchip: in the UK, puppies must be microchipped by 8 weeks old by the breeder. Make sure the database details are transferred to you.
  • Insurance: compare pet insurance early, as conditions that show before cover starts are usually excluded.
  • Parasite control: ask your vet about flea and worm treatment for puppies.

What can wait

  • Adult-size harnesses and coats: your puppy will grow quickly.
  • Expensive beds: many puppies chew them. Start with something washable and cheap to replace.
  • Lots of toys: a few good ones, rotated, are enough.

The first weeks

  • Start toilet training with frequent trips outside, especially after sleeping, eating and play.
  • Begin gentle socialisation: new sounds, people and handling, at your puppy’s pace.
  • Look for a puppy class with a qualified, reward-based trainer.
  • Plan for care when you’re out: see our guide to dog walkers and sitters.

Last updated: 13 October 2026

Frequently asked questions

When can my puppy go outside?
It depends on your puppy’s vaccination schedule. Your vet will tell you when it’s safe to walk in public places.
Should I buy a crate for my puppy?
A crate isn’t essential, but many owners find one helpful for toilet training and as a safe den. Buy the adult size with a divider so it grows with your puppy.
