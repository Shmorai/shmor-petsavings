Dog supplies

Dog supplies: how to choose kit that fits and lasts

The right size and the right material save you money, because you only buy once. Here’s what to look for in the everyday essentials for dogs and puppies.

Collars, ID tags and microchips

  • UK: by law, dogs in public places must wear a collar with a tag showing the owner’s name and address, and dogs must be microchipped with up-to-date details.
  • US and EU: rules vary by state, city and country, but an ID tag and a registered microchip are the best way to get a lost dog home.
  • You should be able to fit two fingers under a flat collar.

Harness or collar for walks?

Many owners use a harness for walking, especially for small dogs, flat-faced breeds and dogs that pull, because it spreads pressure away from the neck. Measure your dog’s chest and neck and check the size chart: a good fit lets the dog move its shoulders freely and won’t slip off backwards. A lead or leash of around 1.2–2 m (4–6 ft) suits everyday walks.

Crates and beds

  • Crates: your dog should be able to stand up, turn around and lie down comfortably. For puppies, a crate with a divider lets you buy the adult size once.
  • Beds: measure your dog lying stretched out. Washable covers save money over time. Older or larger dogs may benefit from supportive (orthopaedic) foam.
  • Chewers: pick tough, chew-resistant beds and avoid stuffing a puppy might swallow.

Toys and enrichment

  • Choose toys too big to swallow and replace them once they break apart.
  • Food puzzles and lick mats slow down fast eaters and keep dogs busy.
  • Avoid cooked bones, which can splinter.
  • Rotate a few toys rather than buying new ones every week: it keeps them interesting.

New dog checklist

Bowls, food, collar and ID tag, lead and harness, bed, crate (optional), poop bags, a brush suited to the coat, toys and chews, a car restraint or crate, and an appointment with a vet. See our full new puppy checklist.

Frequently asked questions

What size crate does my dog need?
One where your dog can stand, turn around and lie down comfortably. For a puppy, buy the adult size with a divider and move it as they grow.
Do dogs have to be microchipped?
In England, Scotland and Wales, yes: dogs must be microchipped and the details kept up to date. Rules elsewhere vary, but microchipping is widely recommended.
